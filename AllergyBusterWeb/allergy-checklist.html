Allergy Safety Checklist
Food & Everyday Products

Stay prepared at home, in the store, at school or work, when eating out, and while traveling, for the food you eat and the products you use.

🛒 Home & Shopping
Read the label every time, even on products you buy often. Recipes change.
Know the 9 major U.S. allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame (added in 2023).
"May contain" warnings are voluntary. No warning doesn't guarantee no cross-contact.
Store safe foods separately; clean surfaces, utensils and hands to avoid cross-contact.
🧴 Personal Care & Home New
Check lotions, shampoos and cosmetics for food-derived ingredients like almond oil, wheat protein or milk.
"Fragrance" or "parfum" can be a mix of ingredients that aren't listed individually.
Read warning labels on cleaners, and never mix cleaning products.
Recheck products labeled "new" or "improved": the formula may have changed.
🏫 School / Work
Share your allergy and emergency action plan with teachers, staff or coworkers.
Keep epinephrine within reach, and make sure people know where it is.
Plan ahead for parties, celebrations and shared snacks.
🍽️ Restaurants & Gatherings
Tell staff about your allergy and ask how food is prepared, including shared fryers, grills and utensils.
At gatherings, ask hosts what's in each dish and serve yourself first.
When ingredients are unclear, skip it.
✈️ Travel
Keep epinephrine in your carry-on, never in checked bags.
Pack safe snacks for delays and research options at your destination.
Bring a copy of your emergency action plan.
🔍 Before You Buy
Scan or photograph the label of anything new, including gifts and seasonal versions of favorites.
Keep a list of products you've already checked and trust.
If a label is missing or unclear, ask the maker, or set it aside.
🚨 Emergency Readiness
Carry two epinephrine doses, as allergists recommend.
Check expiration dates and replace expired devices.
For anaphylaxis, use epinephrine first. Antihistamines don't stop anaphylaxis.
After using epinephrine, call 911 and follow your emergency action plan.
Make sure family, friends and caregivers know how to respond.
Wear medical identification if your doctor recommends it.
